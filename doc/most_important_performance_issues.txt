Buildat: Most important performance issues
==========================================

Issues are ordered by descending importance.

Measured by flying straight and level over infinite terrain in
games/bomber_drone (turn the motor on with M, hold the runway until it is at
flying speed, tap S to climb) with the on-screen profiler up (F9). Debug build,
-O1, 2026-09-08. Numbers are per frame of the client or, where said, of one
section on the server.

The server's own numbers from 2026-09-14 are from a different run and say so
where they appear: a devtest world made from nothing with a client connected
for 45 seconds, under `perf record -F 199 --call-graph dwarf`, Debug build,
-O1. Percentages there are of the whole server process, every thread together.

Fixed, kept here for what the shape of the problem was:

* A region read copied the world a voxel at a time. voxelworld's get_volume()
  walked both volumes with a sampler and copied one voxel per step, and
  luanti's read_region() then walked the answer the same way to turn it into
  words. VoxelVolume::Sampler::movePositiveX() alone was 12% of the whole
  server's CPU. A run along x is contiguous in both volumes, so it is a memcpy
  per plane per row -- VoxelVolume::copy_run_from() -- and reading a row out as
  words is one call rather than one per voxel -- VoxelVolume::read_words().
  The per-voxel walk is still there for any row that is not wholly inside both
  volumes.

  In the profile: movePositiveX() 12.1% -> 2.8% of the whole server, with
  read_words() at 2.6% beside it, and get_volume() out of the top twenty
  altogether. In wall clock, on the same already-generated world with a
  client connected -- a VoxelManip over 49^3 ten times, six rounds -- the
  best round went 62.2 ms -> 54.2 ms and the median did not move: what is
  left in that path is the Lua table the result goes into, the lock, and
  whatever else the server is doing at the time. Debug build, -O1,
  2026-09-14.

  **Measure a world that is already made.** A first run of a save generates
  while it benchmarks and is two to four times slower and far noisier than
  the second run of the same save; comparing one of each says nothing about
  the change. Two measurements in this round had to be thrown away for it.

* The client's spatial update queue was a sorted std::list with a sorted
  std::list as its index, so every put walked thousands of items several times.
  21% of the client's CPU; the whole voxelworld Lua update was 45 ms of every
  frame. A multimap and an unordered_map instead.

* Urho3D's LuaScript ran a full lua_gc collect every frame, walking a heap that
  is large and mostly live. 20 ms per frame. An incremental step instead.

* voxelworld built Bullet collision shapes for every chunk on the server
  whether or not anything simulated against them, and Bullet then kept their
  AABBs up to date forever. 19% of the server's CPU, competing with world
  generation for the module. set_physics_enabled(false) for a world that reads
  its terrain out of the voxel volumes.

Tried and rejected, so that nobody spends the day again:

* Raising voxelworld's chunk buffer limit (m_max_buffers_loaded) from 50 to
  500. It does what it says -- deserialize_volume() under Section::get_buffer()
  goes from 8.8% of the server to under 0.5%, and the zstd decompression with
  it -- and the server then generates *fewer* sections in the same time: 100
  against 132 in a 45 second devtest run. The suspects are
  m_sections_with_loaded_buffers, a sorted vector with an O(n) insert per
  chunk touched, and the locality of ten times the working set. Whatever it
  is, the deserialization was not what the wall clock was waiting for.
  2026-09-14.

Still open, in the order they showed up in a profile after those:

1. update_skylight() and its voxel reads are about 25% of the server's CPU, and
a freshly generated section costs 35-50 ms of it.

**Re-measured 2026-09-14 on devtest: 0.6%, not 25%.** The 25% is a
bomber_drone number and the difference is the game rather than the engine:
bomber_drone writes its terrain into the world without light and every
section is a full unlight-and-spread, while devtest's mapgen hands the light
over with the volume and the write costs no seeds at all. So this is real
where a game builds its world in on_generated -- VoxeLibre does -- and is
nearly free where the generator brings its own light. Measure it on the game
it is about before optimising it. The incremental unlight and
spread machinery is right for an edit, but a whole new section hands it 262144
seeds when nothing above it is loaded yet. A top-down column sweep for that
case would cost a fraction of it.

Chunk serialization and its zlib compression is another 14% of the server, and
a section is serialized more than once on its way in.

**Re-measured 2026-09-14 on devtest: 13.6%, and it holds** -- but two thirds
of it is *deserialization* rather than serialization:
`Section::get_buffer()` reading a chunk back in was 8.8% against 5.6% for
writing one out, with zstd underneath both. That is the same shape as the
note above about serializing more than once: a chunk is written, dropped and
read again inside one section's lifetime. The buffer limit is not the answer
to it -- see "Tried and rejected" above -- and what would be is not going in
a bullet point.

2. The client's software occlusion buffer is about 6.5% of its CPU. Every chunk
is an occluder, LOD chunks included, and the terrain is not a shape that
occludes much of itself from the air.

3. The atlas rebuilds all three of its textures, with their mipmap chains, for
every segment added to it. The segments are bounded per voxel registry, so this
is a startup hitch rather than a steady cost, but batching the SetData of a
whole registry's worth of segments would remove it.

4. Applying the physics box generation result for 32x32x32 voxels into the
physics world is too slow. It is currently split to only two parts; the creation
of the Urho3D::CollisionShapes and constructing the physics stuff in Bullet.
Both can take up to 20us in worst practical case, and cannot be threaded.

This can probably be fixed by splitting the resulting CollisionShapes into
multiple child nodes (assuming the physics system handles that appropriately).

Measured on Dell Precision M6800, release build, 2014-10-18.

5. Creating the CustomGeometry for a 32x32x32 node is too slow. It can take up
to 5000us in the worst practical case and cannot be threaded. It likely cannot
be split into multiple steps (measuring needed).

It is definitely solvable by splitting the node to multiple subvolumes and
creating child nodes out of them.

It might not be worth solving because the time is relatively low.

Measured on Dell Precision M6800, release build, 2014-10-18.
